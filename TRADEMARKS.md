The BYOLLM code in this repository is MIT licensed. **The names "BYOLLM
Cloud" and "byollm.cloud", and our logos, are trademarks of Of Tomorrow,
Inc., and are not licensed by the MIT license.**

Those are two different grants, and separating them is the point of this
file: you may do anything the MIT license allows with the code, and that
permission says nothing about our name.

## You may

- Say truthfully that your site, product, or relay works with BYOLLM, or
  implements the BYOLLM protocol.
- Use the "Connect BYOLLM" button in a conforming integration.
- Fork and modify the code under the MIT terms.

## You may not

- Call a hosted service "BYOLLM Cloud", or use our logos for a service we
  do not operate.
- Present a self-hosted relay as operated by us.
- Use the names in ways that suggest sponsorship we have not given.

If you run your own relay, name it as your own, and say plainly that it is
not operated by BYOLLM Cloud.

## "BYOLLM conformant" describes code, not a deployment

The phrase means a version of software that passes the published
conformance kit — `@byollm/conformance`. **It is a claim about code, and we
do not certify running deployments.**

The distinction is not pedantry. The kit can tell you that an
implementation speaks the protocol correctly; it cannot tell you that the
operator running it is honest about metering, or careful with their
database, or still online tomorrow. Those are properties of a deployment
and of the people behind it, and a conformance badge that appeared to
cover them would be doing the opposite of what this project is for.

If you want to know who you are trusting with a given relay, that is a
different question and it has its own page: [Who you're trusting,
exactly](https://docs.byollm.cloud/trust).
